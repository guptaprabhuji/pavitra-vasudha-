Home Composting DIY

Turn Kitchen Wet Waste into 'Black Gold' (4 Easy Steps)

Composting at home immediately eliminates up to 60% of municipal landfill burden.

nature 100% Odor-Free Method
1 Step 1
Aerated Pot or Compost Bin

Take an earthen terracotta pot or a plastic bucket. Drill small aeration holes around the bottom and sides to ensure proper air flow.

air Ensure Continuous Oxygen Flow
2 Step 2
50:50 Green & Brown Balance

Alternate layers of wet nitrogen-rich waste (vegetable scraps, fruit peels - green) and dry carbon sources (dry leaves, shredded cardboard, cocopeat - brown).

layers Ideal Carbon-to-Nitrogen Ratio
3 Step 3
Moisture Control & Aeration

Turn and aerate the pile once a week. If too dry, sprinkle light water; if too wet or soggy, add dried leaves or wood shavings.

water_drop Damp Sponge Moisture, Not Soggy
4 Step 4
30-Day Rich Humus Ready

In about 4 weeks, you will have dark brown, earthy-smelling organic compost (humus). Sieve and use it directly for plants and gardens.

workspace_premium 100% Pure Organic Plant Nutrition

lightbulb Compost Troubleshooting Guide
1. Foul or sour smell is developing:

Cause: Excess moisture or lack of oxygen. Solution: Add a bowl of dried leaves, sawdust, or cocopeat and thoroughly turn the pile with a garden fork.

2. Tiny fruit flies or gnats appearing:

Cause: Exposed food scraps on top. Solution: Always keep the pile covered with a 1-inch top layer of dry garden soil or dry leaves.

3. Waste has dried out and is not decomposing:

Cause: Insufficient moisture. Solution: Lightly sprinkle diluted buttermilk or water. The lactobacillus bacteria in buttermilk accelerates composting speed threefold.
